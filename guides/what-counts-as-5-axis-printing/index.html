What counts as "5-axis" printing?

In short

5-axis 3D printing is additive manufacturing in which the nozzle and the part re-orient relative to each other during printing: three linear axes (X, Y, Z) plus two rotary axes. The rotary axes may sit in the table, in the head, or be split between both. In 3+2 (indexed) printing the axes re-orient between sections and layers stay flat; in simultaneous 5-axis printing all axes interpolate during deposition, which is what makes true conformal printing — curved, non-planar printing — possible. None of it works without RTCP, the controller compensation that keeps the nozzle tip on the programmed path while the machine tilts.

A printer earns the label when the nozzle and the part can be re-oriented relative to each other while printing — three linear axes (X, Y, Z) plus two rotary axes working together. The rotary axes can live in three places: in the table (a tilting, rotating trunnion), in the head (a swivelling nozzle), or split between both. Each choice changes the machine's stiffness, collision behaviour and usable volume.

Two distinctions matter more than the axis count:

  • 3+2 (indexed) — re-orient between sections, print flat layers within each. Easier, but the layers stay flat and seams appear.
  • Simultaneous 5-axis — all axes interpolate during deposition, orientation changes continuously, and layers can genuinely curve. This is the mode conformal printing needs.
  • RTCP is not optional. Without rotation tool-centre-point compensation, tilting the machine drags the nozzle off its programmed path. Any serious 5-axis claim should name how the controller handles it.

Questions this page answers

Does adding a tilting bed make any printer a 5-axis printer?+

Only if the rotary axes work while printing. A printer earns the 5-axis label when the nozzle and part can be re-oriented relative to each other during deposition — three linear axes plus two rotary axes, coordinated by the controller. A tilting bed that only moves between prints is an accessory, not a fifth axis.

What is the difference between 3+2 and simultaneous 5-axis printing?+

In 3+2 (indexed) printing, the part is re-oriented between sections and each section is printed with flat layers. In simultaneous 5-axis printing, all axes interpolate together while material is deposited, so orientation changes continuously and layers can genuinely curve — the mode conformal printing requires.

Why is RTCP essential for 5-axis printing?+

RTCP (rotation tool centre point) compensates for the nozzle tip's displacement when rotary axes move. Without it, tilting the machine drags the nozzle off its programmed path and conformal toolpaths distort; with it, the tip stays exactly on the path while the machine re-orients around it.
