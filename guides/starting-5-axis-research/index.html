Starting 5-axis research: a practical checklist

In short

Labs starting 5-axis 3D printing research save the most time by deciding four things before hardware arrives: benchmark geometries (a dome, a 70° overhang wall, a curved duct, a curved substrate), the measurements that make claims publishable, a real budget for slicing software or collaboration, and a plan to publish failures as well as results. Hardware without toolpaths is furniture: the slicer, not the frame, is the field's bottleneck.

For labs setting up a non-planar programme, the questions that save the most time are asked before any hardware arrives:

  • Fix your benchmark geometries first — a dome (stair-stepping), a 70° overhang wall (support-free claim), a curved duct (internal access), a print onto a curved substrate. Every route can be compared on the same four parts.
  • Decide what you will measure — surface roughness on curves, interlayer strength along vs. across conformal layers, dimensional error vs. tilt angle. Publishable claims need these numbers.
  • Budget for the slicer, not just the machine — seats, development time or a collaboration agreement; hardware without toolpaths is furniture.
  • Plan to publish the failures — collision cases, singularity behaviour, failed orientations. In a field this young, negative results are the scarcest resource.

Questions this page answers

What should a lab sort out first: the machine or the slicer?+

Decide the slicer question first, and budget for both. A 5-axis machine without non-planar toolpaths prints nothing a 3-axis machine cannot; seats, development time or a collaboration agreement for slicing belong in the budget before hardware arrives.

Which benchmark parts should a 5-axis lab print first?+

Four geometries expose most of the truth: a dome for stair-stepping, a 70° overhang wall for support-free claims, a curved duct for internal access, and a print onto a curved substrate. Every route can be compared honestly on the same four parts.

Why publish failed prints and collision cases?+

Because in a field this young, negative results are the scarcest resource. Collision cases, singularity behaviour and failed orientations save every other group months — and they are exactly the data most teams are tempted to hide.
