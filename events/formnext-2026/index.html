Formnext 2026, viewed through a five-axis lens

The additive-manufacturing calendar's biggest week, filtered to what matters for five-axis and non-planar printing: the stands, the first Defence Summit, and the Awards deadlines. Published 10 November 2026; every date and booth number below is sourced in the sources section.

In short

Formnext 2026 runs 17–20 November 2026 at Messe Frankfurt (Halls 11.0, 12.0, 12.1 and the Portalhaus). For visitors who care specifically about five-axis and non-planar (conformal) printing, the short list is: Layer Performance (non-planar, multi-axis slicing software, Hall 12.1, Booth F111), CMS (six-axis Krearob robotic large-format system with non-planar deposition, Hall 12.0, Stand D51), and the first Formnext Defence Summit on 17 November. The Formnext Awards public vote closes Thursday 19 November at 12:00 local time; winners are announced the same day from 16:10. Everything below is filtered to five-axis and curved-surface relevance — the full show has hundreds of exhibitors, and this page deliberately does not try to list them.

Exhibitors worth your time if you work on five-axis or non-planar printing

This list is short on purpose. Each entry earned its place by a direct connection to multi-axis motion or curved-surface deposition — not by booth size. For how these exhibitors' approaches map onto the six routes to multi-axis printing, see the technology landscape.

Layer Performance — non-planar and multi-axis slicing (Hall 12.1, Booth F111)

Layer Performance GmbH is showing a comprehensively revised version of its Layer Performance Slicer, with expanded capabilities for non-planar and multi-axis additive manufacturing. For the five-axis community this is one of the most relevant software stops at the show: slicing is where non-planar printing succeeds or fails, and dedicated multi-axis toolpath tooling is still rare. Formnext's own pre-show software roundup singles it out for "simplifying the programming of non-planar multi-axis processes."

CMS — Krearob six-axis robotic LFAM, Formnext debut (Hall 12.0, Stand D51)

CMS SpA (SCM Group) is giving its Krearob system its Formnext debut. Krearob puts an extrusion unit on a six-axis industrial robot and is explicitly built for non-planar material deposition and geometries that are difficult on a three-axis Cartesian machine — large thermoplastic tooling, moulds and components for aerospace, automotive and industrial equipment. CMS's gantry-based Kreator A3 is on the same stand for contrast; the robot cell is the one to watch for multi-axis toolpath strategy at large scale.

DMG MORI — five-axis hybrid, adjacent but instructive (Hall 12.0, Booth D139)

Flagged as adjacent: the LASERTEC 65 DED hybrid is a metal directed-energy-deposition machine, not polymer FDM. It earns a mention because it integrates 5-axis simultaneous milling with deposition in one workspace — the same kinematic questions (tool orientation, collision avoidance, RTCP-style control) that five-axis printing has to answer, solved at production scale. Worth a look if your interest is the motion-control side of multi-axis AM.

Research: Fraunhofer joint booth (Hall 11.0, Booth D31)

The Fraunhofer institutes share a joint booth again this year. University and institute groups remain where much of the published non-planar and conformal work originates, so the joint booth is a sensible stop for research-oriented visitors. (Specific five-axis exhibits at the booth were not confirmed at draft time — check the official exhibitor directory before the show.)

Robotic and multi-axis polymer printing is a wider field than the three stands above — vendors such as CEAD and Caracol are the usual reference points for multi-axis robotic extrusion, and software specialists such as Ai Build and Adaxis work on multi-axis toolpath generation. Their 2026 booth details were not verified from official sources at draft time, so they are not listed as confirmed stands here. The first Formnext Defence Summit — Tuesday 17 November

New for 2026, the Formnext Defence Summit takes place on the opening day, organised in cooperation with the Additive Manufacturing Defence Network e.V. Per the organiser, it focuses on additive manufacturing for defence and security applications, with particular emphasis on resilient supply chains, technological sovereignty, and the operational readiness of security-critical industries, bringing together industry, research institutions, government agencies and defence organisations.

Why it appears on a five-axis page: distributed, on-demand spare-part production is one of the summit's stated themes, and real spare parts are rarely flat — the ability to deposit material on curved surfaces and repair existing geometry is directly relevant to that conversation. That is context, not a claim about the summit's agenda; the detailed programme is published by the organiser (formnext.com/program). Note that summit participation has its own registration/accreditation — a standard Formnext ticket does not by itself guarantee admission.

Formnext Awards 2026 — vote before Thursday 19 November, 12:00

The Formnext Awards return in six categories: AMbassador, Design, (R)Evolution, Rookie, Start-up, and Sustainability (the latter supported by Renishaw). The finalists are exhibited at the Awards Showcase (Hall 11.0, Booth F60) and profiled online.

  • Public voting is open now and closes Thursday, 19 November 2026 at 12:00 p.m. (local time). The public online vote counts as one additional jury vote.
  • Winners are announced at the ceremony on 19 November from 16:10 at the Industry & Application Stage (Hall 11.0, Booth D72).
  • Vote and full finalist profiles: formnext.com/awards

An honest note for this page's readers: none of this year's finalists is a dedicated five-axis or non-planar printing entry. The closest adjacencies are in the Start-up category (sallea, which prints soluble cores for lightweight composite parts) and the (R)Evolution category (Fyous's GHOST pin-mould forming system for dental aligners). If multi-axis printing is to appear on this stage in future editions, it will likely come through exactly those routes — tooling and moulding applications where curved geometry is the point.
