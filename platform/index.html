Control & software

The hard part isn't the frame. It's six axes moving as one, and a slicer that thinks in surfaces.

Five-axis printing fails or succeeds in the control stack. Ours pairs a multi-axis controller with RTCP — the tool centre point stays exactly on the programmed path while the machine re-orients around it — with our own curved-surface slicing software, developed alongside the hardware since 2022.

Real multi-axis control, with RTCP

Six axes — X, Y, Z plus the A, B and C rotary axes — interpolate simultaneously under the POCENDER / PCD3D controller, with RTCP (rotation tool centre point) keeping the nozzle tip exactly on the programmed path while the machine re-orients around it. Without RTCP, tilting the part drags the nozzle off its path; with it, conformal toolpaths stay true.

  • Simultaneous interpolation across linear and rotary axes
  • Touchscreen control on the machine
  • Per-axis travel, speed and acceleration parameters

A slicer that thinks in surfaces — and remote operation

Our slicing software generates toolpaths on the part's surfaces instead of in flat planes — the software half of conformal printing, and an active research topic in its own right. The machine itself runs from a touchscreen or remotely through the POCENDER web panel: jog every axis, manage files, update firmware and monitor status from a phone or laptop. On custom builds, the stack extends further:

  • AI-assisted simulation of the print before it runs
  • In-process self-inspection with automatic compensation
  • Full remote control for shared and teaching labs

Who it's for

An exploration, open to the people equipped to explore it.

Conformal printing is not a settled technology — the slicing theory, the motion strategies and the applications are all still being worked out, in public, by a small worldwide community. This platform exists for the people doing that work.

R1

Research institutes & university labs

Groups working on non-planar slicing, multi-axis motion, materials behaviour on curved layers, or conformal applications — who need real five-axis hardware to run experiments on, not just simulations. Joint development and slicer collaboration are how we've worked with institutes and universities since 2022.

R2

Teaching labs

Five-axis machining is standard curriculum; five-axis printing is not — yet. A benchtop machine with an open control stack gives students genuine multi-axis, RTCP and non-planar toolpath concepts on hardware they can see, measure and modify.

R3

Advanced makers & small studios

Experienced builders who have hit the wall of flat-layer printing — support scars on every curve, weak shells, impossible undercuts — and want to experiment with the technology the community itself voted the next core direction of FDM.

R4

Application pioneers

Teams exploring where conformal printing wins first: printing onto existing objects, curved functional surfaces, wearables and prosthetics, aerodynamic and fluid shells. If your application needs a machine variant that doesn't exist yet, that's a custom-build conversation.

Where we honestly are

This is a prototype platform, not a production product. Machines are built one at a time, to order, and the design is still evolving — you will find rough edges, and current builds are not yet at production-level repeatability or polish. We don't publish a catalogue price, a fixed lead time or fleet-support promises, because at this stage those numbers wouldn't be honest.

What you get instead: direct access to the engineers, a machine configured for your questions, and a say in where the platform goes next. The labs and makers who join at this stage are the ones shaping what five-axis conformal printing becomes.
